A step is what the cook does before looking at the recipe again. It has one focus: one thing being worked on, in one place.

Start a new step when
- the cook turns to something else: another ingredient or group of ingredients, another pot, bowl or tool, another place (from the board to the pan, from the pan to the oven);
- a cooking, baking, resting or other waiting time is over: a step ends with its timed phase, nothing follows it in the same step;
- the step would otherwise be longer than about two sentences of instructions.

Keep in one step
- actions done in one go on the same thing: "Peel and chop the onion.", "Drain and rinse the beans.";
- adding something and what is done with it right away: "Add the flour and stir until combined.", "Put the onions in the pan and cook for 15 minutes.";
- a change of setting and the action it serves: "Lower the heat and simmer for 20 minutes.";
- a timing lead-in and its action: "After 2 to 3 minutes, add the ginger.";
- actions done at the same time: "Cook for 10 minutes, stirring occasionally.";
- a tip, warning or explanation and the action it belongs to. A sentence without an action is never a step of its own.

A step therefore holds at most one timed cooking or waiting phase. "Slice the onions, put them in a pan and cook for 15 minutes." is two steps: "Slice the onions." and "Put the onions in the pan and cook for 15 minutes."

Every step names what it acts on. Only the thing handled in the step directly before may be left unnamed or called "it": "Simmer for a minute." is clear right after the step that made the sauce, "Pour into the dish." is not clear when the reader has to work out what is poured.
